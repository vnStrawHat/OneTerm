## Source

The audit is the authoritative input. It is a read-only survey of
`crates/{vt, terminal, terminal-view, ssh, local-shell, pty, tools, agent-ui}` plus `crates/core`
on `main` @ `782bb77`, written after the `IN-0029` engine migration and the `IN-0031` startup fix.
Every number quoted below is its number; this intake adds no new measurement.

## Requested Outcome

The terminal crates keep the boundaries the `IN-0029` migration re-derived, and shed the residue
that migration left behind. Concretely, after this intake:

- No type or method survives only because a comment said "delete me with the packet that moves my
  call site" — those packets have shipped.
- `oneterm-vt` holds no atomic and no interior mutability, so its crate doc is true rather than
  contradicted three lines later.
- `oneterm-vt` and `oneterm-terminal` publish roughly the API their consumers actually name,
  not three times it.
- The two remaining per-frame full-viewport scans in the render path cost change, not viewport
  area — the shape `DEC-0015` removed from the render copy one layer down.
- The parity corpus lives in the crate that reads it, and the one genuinely duplicated piece of
  backend code has one home.

**No crate is created, merged, moved or removed.** No behaviour changes anywhere. If any packet in
this intake alters observable terminal behaviour, that packet is wrong.

### The two per-frame viewport scans (§4, H1 and H2)

- **H1** — `crates/terminal-view/src/render/plan_cache.rs:143-152` runs `url_masks_into` over the
  whole viewport whenever *any* row is dirty, then compares the whole previous mask. At 45×160
  that is about 7 200 cells × `|PREFIXES|` comparisons per changed frame, plus a second
  7 200-byte compare, however few rows changed.
- **H2** — `crates/terminal/src/content.rs:56-67` (`last_content_row`) walks rows bottom-up and
  does **three interner lookups per cell**, once per rendered frame, under the plain
  `TerminalHandle::lock()`. Its worst case is the common idle case: a fresh shell with a prompt on
  row 0 scans the entire viewport every frame. `handle.rs:112-115` justifies taking the plain lock
  on the premise that `terminal_info` is O(1) under it; that premise is false today.

The audit is explicit that neither cost was measured (§(d).1): *"My claims are complexity
arguments from reading, not measurements … neither should be sold to the owner as a known win."*
That is why `US-0092` carries a counted-work proof of its own rather than inheriting a number.

### Cross-backend duplication (§1.2)

Total duplicated code between `local-shell` and `ssh`: **about 35 lines** — the
`fetch_update(checked_add().filter(<= BUDGET))` byte-budget idiom against two identically valued
4 MiB constants (`local-shell/src/event_loop.rs:120-140`, `ssh/src/transport.rs:121-134`) — plus
the shape of the one macro call each makes. Everything else in the two read loops is genuinely
protocol-specific: the local loop holds the engine guard across reads and caps each at
`MAX_LOCKED_READ`; the SSH loop **cannot** hold a guard across reads at all.

### Doc rot (§2.3) and stale prose (§2.2)

Seven dead doc references (`D1`–`D7`) and seven comments citing deleted files (`R1`–`R7`).
`python scripts/check-doc-paths.py` passes and is blind to all of them twice over: its `DOCUMENTS`
list excludes `docs/terminal-backend.md`, and its regex only sees back-ticked tokens, so ASCII
tree diagrams are invisible.

### `terminal-diagnostics` (§2.4)

Declared by four crates, enabled by **nothing automated** — `grep -rn terminal-diagnostics
.github/ scripts/` returns 0 hits and no CI step passes `--all-features`. It guards about 24 `cfg`
sites, 18 of them bare `#[cfg(feature = …)]` inside `crates/local-shell/src/event_loop.rs`'s
638-line read loop, which therefore is never type-checked or clippy'd by CI. About 200 lines.

## Project Impact

- **Surfaces:** none. No user-visible behaviour, no rendering change, no key or mouse handling
  change, no persisted format, no settings, no CLI. `US-0092` changes *how much work* a frame
  does, never what it draws.
- **Contracts:** `TerminalSession` keeps its method surface (`US-0091` changes who implements it,
  not what it is). `SessionFactory`, `SftpBackend`, `AppServices`, `WorkspaceCommands` and every
  registered dock panel name are untouched. `oneterm-vt`'s and `oneterm-terminal`'s **Rust**
  public surfaces shrink; both are workspace-internal, so no external consumer exists.
- **Crate graph:** unchanged. R1–R12 all keep holding, and **R7 gets stronger** — moving `Demand`
  out removes `oneterm-vt`'s only atomic, making `crates/vt/src/lib.rs:4`'s "holds no lock and no
  interior mutability" true instead of contradicted at `:7`. `crates/tools` gains 3.2 MB of data
  it already reads, and no dependency.
- **Users:** none directly. Indirectly, `US-0092` reduces per-frame CPU on idle and lightly
  changing screens, which is where a terminal spends most of its life.
- **Systems:** no schema, no migration, no secret, no external effect, no packaging change.
- **Tests:** every existing test must keep passing untouched except where a packet names the file
  it rewrites. The test-count baselines are recorded per packet.

## Architecture and Boundary Questions

- **Runtime and owning boundary:** unchanged, and that is the point. `crates/vt` stays the
  gpui-free, OneTerm-free engine leaf; `crates/terminal` stays the gpui-free adapter holding the
  lock, the event policy and the view support; `crates/local-shell` and `crates/ssh` stay separate
  backends; `crates/terminal-view` stays the gpui feature. The audit's Option C — folding `pty`,
  `local-shell` and `ssh` into `terminal`, and `agent-ui` into `terminal-view` — is **rejected**;
  the reasoning is in the High-Level Design.
- **Data ownership and lifecycle:** `Demand` moves crate, keeping its type and semantics
  (`Arc<AtomicUsize>` raised by the view, read by the pump); its owner is already the policy in
  `crates/terminal/src/handle.rs`. The parity corpus moves directory and keeps its `NOTICE`; its
  content is frozen and byte-identical after the move.
- **Auth, security, privacy, or audit:** none. No packet touches host keys, credentials,
  `known_hosts`, the paste and OSC sanitisers, or the clipboard policy.
- **External systems and side effects:** none. No process spawn, no network, and no file written
  by the application changes. `US-0093` moves files in the repository only.
- **Public interfaces and compatibility:** the Rust surfaces of `oneterm-vt` and
  `oneterm-terminal` shrink. Both are workspace-internal crates with no published consumer, so the
  only compatibility question is inside this repository, and
  `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` answers it exhaustively.
  `TerminalSession` and every contract in `docs/PROJECT.md` § Important Boundaries keep their
  shape.

- [ ] **`terminal-diagnostics`: gate it in CI, or delete it?** The audit (§2.4) found it declared

  200 lines across about 24 `cfg` sites — 18 of them bare `#[cfg(feature = …)]` threaded through

  type-checked or clippy'd by CI. (`crates/ssh/src/transport.rs`'s nine sites are
  `#[cfg(any(test, feature = …))]`, so `cargo test` does compile them.) The audit offers two
  honest choices and does not pick: add one CI step, or delete the feature.

  `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --features oneterm-app/terminal-diagnostics -- -D warnings`
  in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` and both `scripts/ci-local.*`. One line in three files buys
  compile coverage for diagnostics that exist precisely for an incident, against a 200-line
  deletion that is unrecoverable at the moment they are wanted. Owner: `US-0090`'s implementer,
  who must record which branch was taken and why. **Not blocking** — `US-0090`'s other work is
  independent of it.

  render path and explicitly did not audit `shapes.rs` (1 341) or `row_plan.rs` (1 038) (§(d).6).

- [ ] **Whether the `corpus/oneterm/` recording should stay adjacent to the engine for provenance**
