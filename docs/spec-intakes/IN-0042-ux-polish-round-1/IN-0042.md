## Source

Two owner messages, both written in Vietnamese and recorded here in English (this
repository's contributor text is English-only; see `scripts/check-english.py`):

2. **2026-09-16, the ruling, after reading the result.** Read out: *handle all of S, M and
   L, then make a report with before/after images.* "S", "M" and "L" are the walkthrough's
   own effort buckets — the fourteen quick wins (P1–P14, "S") and the eleven larger items

The walkthrough itself is the authoritative finding list and is copied into this intake at
`research/ux-walkthrough-2026-09-16.md`, with its 67 screenshots in `research/before/`

records 34 findings (F1–F34: 6 high, 17 medium, 11 low) and 25 proposals (P1–P25), each
naming the crate, the file and the owning document it touches, plus a "settled by owner

The walk was made read-only against `main @2f12628a` with a locked desktop, so every
screenshot is a `PrintWindow` capture of the walk's own process and every input was a
posted `WM_*` message. Two consequences carry into this intake's verification plans: no
Ctrl/Shift chord and no double-click could be exercised, so `F31`/`P18` is a code read and
tab rename was never driven.

## Requested Outcome

Every proposal P1–P25 is implemented, each in a packet that can be verified on its own,
and the round then closes with a before/after report: the same 67 scenes re-captured
after the work and set beside the frames in `research/before/`, with a per-finding verdict.

Nothing in the walkthrough's "settled by owner decision" list is reopened: the `+` menu's
row order, the session colour squares, the 1 px Space border, split persistence, channel
membership, the absent Edit/View/Help menus, password non-persistence, asynchronous

## Project Impact

- **Surfaces.** Almost every visible surface the application has: the built-in themes, the
  centre tab bar and its `+` menu, split Spaces, the status bar, the right dock and its
  width, the SSH Sessions tree, the quick-connect and full session dialogs, the Settings
  window (General, Appearance, About, Key Bindings, the sidebar), the SFTP browser in both
  its docked and dual-pane forms, and the Agent panel.
- **Contracts.** Six current-state documents describe behaviour this round changes; they
  are listed below. None is redefined — each gains or amends the sentences that describe
  the surface a packet moves.
- **Users.** Every user. One change is visible without being asked for: `US-0123` moves
  four shipped default key bindings off single-Ctrl keys, which is why it carries its own
  decision record and an owner acceptance (see Lane, below).
- **Systems.** No new external effect, no new dependency, no credential handling change.
  Two persisted documents are read or written differently — `docks.json` (the right dock
  width, `US-0113`; the SFTP table column state, `US-0124`) and `ui_config.json` (the
  key-binding override map, `US-0123`) — and neither gains a schema version bump on the
  evidence so far; each owning packet confirms that before implementing.
- **Risk shape.** Normal lane throughout. The round is wide rather than deep: many small
  surfaces, each independently provable, with no authorization, migration, secret or
  external-effect surface among them apart from the key-binding default change.

### Lane

Normal, for every packet. `US-0123` is the one exception in process rather than in lane: it
changes **shipped default key bindings**, which the walkthrough itself flagged as needing a
decision record (`P18`, "this is a shipped-default change"). It therefore needs
`DEC-0018-app-shortcuts-leave-single-ctrl-keys-to-the-terminal.md` accepted **and an
explicit owner acceptance of the new defaults** before it merges. It stays in the normal
lane because it touches no authorization, data or external-effect surface, so no detail
design is required; the decision record carries the rationale instead.

## Architecture and Boundary Questions

- **Runtime and owning boundary.** Each packet stays inside the crate that owns the surface
  it changes: `crates/theme` (`US-0111`), `crates/workspace` (`BUG-0067`, `US-0112`,
  `US-0113`), `crates/terminal-view` (`US-0114`–`US-0117`), `crates/session-ui`
  (`BUG-0068`, `US-0118`, `US-0119`, `BUG-0069`, `US-0120`), `crates/settings-ui`
  (`US-0121`–`US-0123`), `crates/sftp-ui` (`US-0124`), `crates/agent-ui` (`US-0125`). The
  one cross-feature reach is `US-0114`: the `+` menu lives in `crates/terminal-view` and
  the full session dialog in `crates/session-ui`, and `oneterm-session-ui` already depends
  on `oneterm-terminal-view` — the single same-layer edge R5 allows — so the reverse edge
  would be a cycle (R1). It goes through a new `WorkspaceCommands` fn pointer in
  `crates/state/src/commands.rs`, registered in `crates/app/src/init.rs`, exactly as
  `open_new_session_dialog` and `open_saved_ssh_session` already are (R10).
- **Data ownership and lifecycle.** `docks.json` keeps its owner (`crates/state`'s
  `DockDocument`); the right dock's width lives in the kit's flattened `dock_fields`, so
  `US-0113` clamps what is written and what is applied rather than adding a field.
  `ui_config.json`'s `key_bindings` map is sparse by construction — `overrides_from_effective`
  omits every entry equal to the built-in default — which is what makes `US-0123`'s
  migration rule expressible at all. `ssh_session.json` is unchanged by every packet;
  `US-0118` saves through the existing store API and adds no field.
- **Auth, security, privacy, or audit.** No packet reads, stores, logs or displays a
  credential. `US-0118` keeps a typed password **in the open dialog's memory** across a
  failed connect and nowhere else, which leaves `DEC-0001` (passwords are never persisted)
  intact; the packet states that explicitly and its acceptance checks the store on disk.
  Host-key approval (praised in the walkthrough) is untouched.
- **External systems and side effects.** One addition: `US-0121` puts a "Check for Updates"
  button on the Settings About page, which runs the update check the About **dialog**
  already runs. Same action, same endpoint, no new network destination.
- **Public interfaces and compatibility.** `WorkspaceCommands` gains one field
  (`US-0114`); it is built only at the composition root and in test doubles, so every
  crate's test doubles must be updated with it. No action name changes, no persisted format
  changes. The one user-visible contract break is `US-0123`'s default key bindings, which
  is the reason for `DEC-0018`.
- **Upstream boundary — the two packets that reach code the project may not patch.**
  `gpui-component` is consumed from crates.io with no `[patch]` section (`Cargo.toml`), and
  `docs/PROJECT.md` forbids vendoring or patching it. Two proposals need behaviour that
  lives inside it:
  - `P16`/`US-0122` — `gpui_component::setting::Settings` owns the sidebar-to-group scroll,
    so "clicking a sub-item scrolls to its group" is upstream behaviour.
  - `P17`/`US-0116` — `gpui_component::dock::TabGroup` owns the tab strip, its clipping and
    its overflow, so a tab context menu and a tab list are upstream surfaces.

  Neither packet may be closed by saying "upstream". Each must either deliver a OneTerm-side
  solution — a page wrapper that owns its own scroll for `US-0122`, a right-click handler on
  the tab content OneTerm does render for `US-0116` — **or** record the precise limit: which
  upstream item blocks it, the file and line in `reference/gpui-kit/` that shows why, what
  the OneTerm-side attempt cost, and the follow-up raised upstream. A packet that records
  the limit still ships whatever part is reachable from this side, and says in its Gaps
  which finding it leaves standing.

## Open Decisions and Questions

- [ ] **`US-0123` — the new default key bindings.** Owner acceptance required before merge,
      alongside `DEC-0018`. The question is not whether single-Ctrl keys should move (the
      walkthrough's argument that `ctrl-s`/`ctrl-q`/`ctrl-g` are XOFF/XON/BEL stands) but
      which replacements ship: `P18` proposes `ctrl-shift-s`/`ctrl-shift-n`, `ctrl-shift-q`,
      `f1` or `ctrl-shift-/`, and dropping or moving `ctrl-g`. Blocking `US-0123` only.
- [ ] **`US-0116` and `US-0122` — how far the OneTerm side reaches.** Each packet's first
      action is a reference read in `reference/gpui-kit/` to establish whether the outcome
      is reachable without patching the kit. If it is not, the packet ships the reachable
      part and records the limit; it does not silently narrow its acceptance. Not blocking
      the round.
- [ ] **`US-0121` — where network settings land.** `P12` proposes a new "Network" page for
      Proxy URL and Verify Certificates. Whether that page is new or those two fields move
      into an existing group is the packet's call, made against `docs/auto-update.md`; the
      acceptance is that they are no longer under About. Not blocking.
- [ ] **`US-0113` — the clamp shape.** Proportional width, a percentage clamp, or a clamp
      plus auto-collapse below a threshold. The packet decides against the measurement in
      `research/before/50-narrow-900.png`; the acceptance is that the terminal keeps the
      majority of a ~900 px window. Not blocking.
