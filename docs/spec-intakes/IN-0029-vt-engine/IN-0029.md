## Requested Outcome

OneTerm owns its VT engine. Two new Apache-2.0 workspace crates replace the vendored
`alacritty_terminal` + `vte` pair:

- **`crates/pty` (`oneterm-pty`)** — pseudo-console transport only. On Windows it reproduces
  today's resolution order: the **bundled** `conpty.dll` + `OpenConsole.exe` pair loaded with
  `LoadLibraryW` first, `kernel32`'s `CreatePseudoConsole` only as the fallback when the files
  are missing. The bundle is load-bearing — the inbox `conhost.exe` on Windows 11 24H2 swallows
  Sixel DCS payloads, so IN-0028's graphics survive only through the bundled host (`DEC-0013`,
  being rewritten by IN-0030 to say exactly this, with a version-bump script and a manifest).
  `openpty` on Unix. Extracted first so it survives the engine work independently:
  `crates/ssh` needs no PTY at all and `crates/tools` needs a PTY with no grid.
- **`crates/vt` (`oneterm-vt`)** — parser, dispatch, cell, grid, scrollback, reflow, damage,
  render state, events and graphics. A leaf crate with no OneTerm dependency and no gpui.

When the intake closes, `vendor/`, `vendor/patches/` (823 patch lines), `vendor/refresh.sh`,
its CI job and the `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` § 2 rows are gone; adding a VT capability is a
change to a first-party crate under normal review instead of a patch rebase against an
upstream in maintenance mode.

The case for the intake is **extension cost, not speed**. The measured headroom at realistic
shell and SSH rates is two to three orders of magnitude
([`research/perf-baseline.md`](research/perf-baseline.md) § 4, § 5.9); no packet may claim a
throughput win as its outcome. What the rewrite buys is that Sixel, Kitty graphics, kitty
keyboard, mode 2027, mode 2048, XTVERSION and the OSC 9;7 agent channel stop being patches
against someone else's tree.

## Architecture and Boundary Questions

- **Runtime and owning boundary.** `oneterm-vt` is single-threaded and `Send`, never `Sync`:
  it takes `&mut self` and owns no lock. The adapter in `crates/terminal` keeps
  `Arc<FairMutex<Terminal>>` and decides the sync strategy, so the engine stays testable
  without a runtime. `oneterm-pty` owns file handles and is driven by the caller's poll loop;
  it spawns no threads of its own on Unix and only the two unavoidable pipe-service threads on
  Windows.
- **Data ownership and lifecycle.** The engine owns cells, interned styles, extras, graphemes,
  decoded graphics pixels and the event arena. Pixels leave through a drain, exactly once, and
  their placements are released with an explicit event. The embedder owns the render state
  buffer it passes in and the watermark inside it. Row identity (`RowId`) is assigned by the
  engine, never by a consumer, and survives everything except reflow, which returns a remap.
- **Auth, security, privacy, audit.** Every byte reaching the engine is untrusted: it comes
  from a PTY or an SSH channel. The three unbounded buffers in the current engine (OSC payload,
  per-cell zero-width list, `Row::new(0)`) are designed out with explicit caps that **truncate
  rather than error**. OSC 52 clipboard read stays default-off and the decision stays in
  `crates/terminal/src/security_policy.rs`; the engine only reports the request. Titles and OSC
  payloads keep their existing sanitisation at the adapter. No `unsafe` is planned outside
  `oneterm-pty`'s Windows FFI, and every block there carries its invariant.
- **External systems and side effects.** The pseudo-console — the bundled
  `conpty.dll` + `OpenConsole.exe` pair when present, the OS `kernel32` implementation otherwise
  (`DEC-0013`, owned by IN-0030) — the SSH transport, and the clipboard through the existing
  policy. Nothing else.
- **Public interfaces and compatibility.** `TerminalSession`, `SessionFactory` and
  `TerminalContent` are the seam and keep their names through the migration; their bodies are
  rewritten. `DEC-0014` records that call sites may change anywhere in the workspace. Wire
  compatibility (what a remote program sees) changes only where the deliberate-deviation table
  in [`low-level-design/dispatch-and-modes.md`](low-level-design/dispatch-and-modes.md) says
  so, and DA1 is the one answer users' programs will notice.

### What remains before the merge to main

The engine work itself is complete: `US-0071` - `US-0088` are merged and independently verified
on `feat/vt-engine`. Three things stand between that branch and `main`.

1. **The tab title (the one FAIL in the acceptance walk).** `title`, a raw `OSC 0` and a raw
   `OSC 2` all leave the tab label at `Terminal`, while an `OSC 9;4` in the same byte stream
   paints the progress bar and the app's OSC log shows only `7` and `133` — so the sequence never
   reaches the parser and the evidence points **outside** the VT engine, at the ConPTY host.
   The consumer chain above the engine is intact and unit-tested. Being root-caused in its own
   worktree; whichever packet it reopens or opens owns the fix, and this intake does not close
   until that packet does.
2. **A green `pwsh scripts/ci-local.ps1` on the merge candidate** — the only intake-level
   acceptance line still open above.
3. **Owner sign-off on the walk**, with the two knowingly retained items visible in it: the
   `cat`-through-ConPTY Sixel byte loss that IN-0028 already recorded as a host defect, and the

(one unreachable, one without stored credentials) and the loopback `sftp-dev-server` answers
`shell_request` with an echo only. `crates/ssh` shares the backend loop `crates/local-shell`
exercises, so the risk this leaves is the transport, not the engine; it needs one real host.

## Design summary and open decisions for the owner

**Delegation and rulings.** On 2026-09-12 the owner delegated items 1-17 below to the coordinator
with the directive: *"Think as a new build, not as a copy of the old one. The new engine must solve
the problems that exist in the old one; extensibility, optimality and performance come first."* The
coordinator's rulings, recorded so no packet reopens them:

| Item | Ruling |
| --- | --- |
| 1, 3-5, 7-17 | **Kept as designed.** |
| **2** | **Reversed: correctness first, not parity-first.** Quirks the recordings pin are implemented spec-correct from the start; each is a `C`-row with its affected recordings, and the parity harness carries per-recording, cell-level expected differences. `US-0086` shrinks to additive features. |
| **6** | **Resolved:** no further patch to the old fork — the new parser bounds OSC and DCS by design (`parser.md`) — and the OSC 9;7 collision is fixed by moving the agent protocol to a free code in the new packet `US-0088`, with `9;7` kept as a deprecated alias for one release. |

The directive is answered in full by the HLD's
[Problems of the current engine](high-level-design.md) table, which is the checklist the finished
engine is reviewed against.

Everything below is a choice the design had to make, ranked by how expensive it is to reverse
later. Each states the choice, the reason, and what overruling it costs.

**1. `oneterm-vt-parser` is a module, not a crate.**
Chosen: `crates/vt/src/parser/` inside `oneterm-vt`, with a narrow internal `Dispatch` trait so
the state machine stays swappable. Reason: `docs/agents/code-style.md` says to extract a crate
only after multiple crates need it, and structure.md § 4 says to open an issue before adding a
crate; only `oneterm-vt` will ever consume it, publishing it standalone is a non-goal, and a
separate crate would force `pub` on items that want `pub(crate)`, adding a manifest, a lint
block, a graph row and a doc row for zero callers. Overruling this is cheap **now** (a module
becomes a crate mechanically) and expensive after `US-0076`, once dispatch and parser share
types freely. If the owner wants the parser publishable, say so before `US-0073`.

**2. Correctness first — reversed by the owner on 2026-09-12.**
The design originally said parity-first: reproduce every quirk the 45 recordings pin, so a red gate
always means a bug. The owner reversed it — *"think as a new build, not as a copy of the old
one"* — so the quirks are implemented **spec-correct from the start**: `DCH` shifts left by `n`,
`ED 1` clears row 0, a short region scroll rotates before blanking, insert mode repairs wide pairs,
`CPR` honours origin mode, `RIS` resets the palette, `OSC 4` applies complete pairs, `? 47` /
`? 1047` / `? 1048` and `DECSTR` and `CSI ? 5 W` work, and blink and overline are stored. Each is a
`C`-row in a corrections table naming the recordings it affects, and the parity harness carries
**per-recording, cell-level** expected differences keyed by that id — a declared window that does
not differ fails too, so the gate survives the reversal. `US-0086` shrinks to genuinely additive
features (DECXCPR, XTVERSION, `modifyOtherKeys` reporting, reverse wrap).

**3. `WRAPLINE` becomes a row flag, not a flag on the last cell.**
Chosen: `RowFlags::WRAPPED`. Reason: it is what the flag actually means, it survives cell
rewrites that today can silently clear it, and reflow reads it once per row instead of probing
the last cell. Cost of overruling: a bit in the cell and a different reflow inner loop. The
recordings compare rendered content, so the gate is unaffected either way.

**4. Mode 2026 is honoured by skipping frames, never by buffering bytes.**
Chosen: the engine applies everything and `render_update` returns `Unchanged` while an update
is open, with a 150 ms refresh timeout and a 1 s watchdog. Reason: `vte`'s 2 MiB sync buffer is
a memory amplifier any SSH session can aim at us, and Ghostty moved the other way for the same
reason. Cost of overruling: the buffered model is a strictly larger change and reintroduces the
byte-exact 8-byte memcmp detection quirk (trap 41).

**5. Interning GC is kept for graphemes only; the style sweep is deleted.**
Grapheme sequences are attacker-reachable and unbounded, so that arena keeps a mark-and-sweep GC
by remap. Styles do not: the ladder is reuse, insert, fall back to the default style with one
warning, and **a style id never moves once assigned**. That was the review's R-52, and it also
removes the race it identified in R-14 — a render copy taken under the lock can never be
invalidated by later table maintenance. Overruling it means reintroducing a full mutating walk of
both screens to recover ids nobody has run out of.

**6a. No patch to the old fork; the new parser is the fix (owner ruling, 2026-09-12).**
The unbounded `osc_raw` stays exploitable until the new parser lands rather than being capped with
a sixth vendor patch. The bound is designed into `parser.md` § "OSC: streamed with explicit caps",
and the exposure window is the migration itself (HLD table rows P1-P3).

**6b. OSC 9;7 moves to a free code in `US-0088`.**
The agent channel keeps working through the registration table meanwhile; `9;7` stays as a
deprecated alias for one release.

**6. The engine is `Send` and not `Sync`; the lock lives in the adapter.**
Chosen: `oneterm-vt` exposes `&mut self` and no interior mutability; `crates/terminal` keeps
`Arc<FairMutex<Terminal>>` and owns the demand/yield handshake. Reason: it keeps the engine
unit-testable with no runtime and lets the locking strategy change without touching the engine.
Cost of overruling: moving the lock inside the engine would make every future consumer inherit
our fairness policy.

**7. `parking_lot::FairMutex` replaces `alacritty_terminal::sync::FairMutex`, plus an explicit
demand signal.**
Chosen: `parking_lot` is already in the lock file; the `lease()` reservation that alacritty
offers is not used anywhere in OneTerm today (`research/api-surface.md` § 3.9), and Ghostty's
demand/yield handshake solves the starvation case that fairness alone does not. Cost of
overruling: porting alacritty's two-mutex fair lock is about 40 lines with attribution.

**8. `RowId` names a position, not a piece of content.**
The ring slot is the id, so an id stays with its content across scrollback pushes, `RIS`, `ED 2`
and viewport scrolling, but **not** across `IL`, `DL`, `SU`, `SD` or an in-region scroll — which
is the normal case inside tmux and vim. Anchoring is therefore an engine service: one tracked
anchor list holds the saved cursor, both selection anchors, every graphics placement and every
mark, and every row-moving primitive plus reflow updates it. `DEC-0015` says this explicitly now.
The alternative — ids that name content — decouples the id from the slot, turning every row access
into a lookup, and still needs the same bookkeeping.

**9. Grapheme cells cap at 16 codepoints.**
Chosen: kitty uses 24, Ghostty 64 with 4-codepoint chunks. 16 covers every ZWJ family and
skin-tone sequence in practice and keeps the arena small. Overruling is a constant.

**10. The scratch-grid cursor probe is replaced, not ported.**
Chosen: `KeepViewportTop` measures conhost's cursor row by reflowing the viewport-top-to-cursor
row range through the same reflow iterator, inside the engine, with no grid clone and no double
`swap_alt`. Reason: the current 61-line dance exists only because `Term` has no
`inactive_grid()` accessor. Risk: it must reproduce the same number the current probe produces,
which is what the `keep_viewport_top_*` tests pin. If it cannot, the fallback is to port the
probe verbatim behind the same policy enum, which is a contained loss.

**11. `crates/ssh` loses `alacritty_terminal` and gains `oneterm-vt` directly.**
It only ever used `Term` + `FairMutex`. The alternative — routing SSH's grid through
`crates/terminal` — is cleaner on paper but would change a second crate's structure during the
swap. Overruling costs one packet's worth of moves.

**12. The corpus is vendored as recordings plus expectations blessed by the OLD engine.**
The 46 MB of upstream `grid.json` is not committed; instead `US-0072` generates a cell-exact,
run-length, nothing-trimmed `grid.expect` **and** a OneTerm `state.expect` (cursor, modes,
palette, title, tab stops) with the engine being replaced, cross-checks the first against
upstream's JSON once, and freezes both. The new engine never blesses; `vt-corpus bless` refuses
without `--deviation <row-id>`. Overruling this by letting the new engine bless would make the
gate prove self-consistency and nothing else.

**13. `proptest` becomes a dev-dependency.**
Already in `Cargo.lock`. Reflow is the one component where the literature says property tests
are the difference between working and "known to fail for an unknown reason". Overruling means
hand-written reflow cases only.

**14. Kitty graphics, kitty keyboard encoding, win32-input-mode, mode 2027, mode 2048 and mode
2031 are out of scope.**
Each is a later intake. This intake only guarantees that the extras table, the APC/DCS plumbing,
the mode table and the keyboard flag stack leave room for them. Mode 2027 joined the list on the
review's R-56: the storage decision (intern, cap at 16 codepoints) and `cluster_width()` ship now,
the print path and the ConPTY glyph-width axis do not — which also removes R-38, because a flag
fixed at `CreatePseudoConsole` time can never follow a mode set afterwards. `? 9001` is likewise
**recognised and inert** rather than unimplemented (R-36), because conhost sends it unprompted on
every local Windows session. Overruling any of these widens `US-0086` materially.

**15. Dual-form rows are deferred (review R-51).**
Every operation that matters coerces a uniform row to a general one anyway, and the memory win the
design claims comes from `Option<Row>` lazy allocation, which is independent. v1 is one row
representation behind a `RowRef` / `RowMut` API, so adding the uniform form later is internal. The
gate is the tier-5 RSS measurement `US-0072` produces.

**16. `VtEvent::Passthrough` is cut from v1 (review R-35).**
Windows Terminal's re-emit mechanism exists because conhost is a relay; OneTerm is the terminal at
the end of the chain, and no consumer could be named. Unhandled sequences are dropped and counted,
exactly as today. It returns in `US-0086` only if SSH-to-ConPTY bridging is ever built.

**17. The migration is a shim plus one packet per consumer group (review R-42).**
`US-0081` puts the new engine under a `LegacySnapshot` that produces today's `TerminalContent`, so
its acceptance is "workspace green, zero behaviour diff, no consumer changed". `US-0082`-`US-0085`
then move one consumer group each. Overruling this returns to a single packet that rewrites four
crates and 45 tests at once, which `docs/HARNESS.md` does not allow.
